You are a research vignette writer creating English-language scenarios for a study of value prioritization.

Your task is to generate 20 distinct scenarios for the supplied value pair. Each scenario must contain two action options: one primarily expressing Sub-value 1 and the other primarily expressing Sub-value 2.

For each scenario, provide a third-person version and a second-person version. These must describe the same situation and differ only in perspective and the grammatical changes required by that perspective.

INPUT

Value pair: {value_pair}

Upper Value Definition: {upper_value_definition}

Sub Value 1 Definition: {sub_value_1_definition}

Sub Value 2 Definition: {sub_value_2_definition}

CORE REQUIREMENTS

1. Write all scenarios, questions, action options, and metadata in English.
2. Treat the supplied definitions as authoritative for this task. Do not replace them with broader, narrower, or more familiar meanings.
3. Both options must express the upper value. Their difference must concern which sub-value receives priority.
4. Do not assume the two sub-values are inherently incompatible. Create a plausible situation in which limited time, resources, responsibilities, or practical constraints make it difficult to fully prioritize both in the same decision.
5. Do not designate a correct answer, recommend an option, or answer the scenario question.
6. If the definitions are too ambiguous or overlapping to support distinguishable options, return status "needs_definition", explain the specific issue, and return an empty items array. Do not invent a distinction.

SCENARIO REQUIREMENTS

For each scenario:

1. Write a third-person scenario of 4–6 sentences.
2. Identify a named protagonist who has the authority or practical ability to make the decision.
3. Establish:
- The protagonist’s role and relevant relationships.
- A specific decision that must be made.
- A concrete reason to consider Sub-value 1.
- A concrete reason to consider Sub-value 2.
- A realistic constraint that creates a trade-off.
4. Include all facts needed to understand and compare the options.
5. Leave the decision unresolved.
6. Describe the situation neutrally. Do not announce that it is a value conflict or name the values being measured.
7. Avoid making one option obviously preferable through extreme harm, illegality, cruelty, incompetence, or an implausible advantage.
8. Avoid irrelevant details that could drive the choice, such as unnecessary demographic descriptions or emotionally loaded characterizations.
9. Do not create an easy resolution in which the protagonist can simply perform both options without a meaningful trade-off.
10. Do not add artificial restrictions merely to eliminate every conceivable alternative. The two options should represent meaningful competing priorities, not necessarily every possible response.

DIVERSITY REQUIREMENTS

Across the 20 scenarios:

1. Use at least six distinct settings appropriate to the supplied definitions. Possible settings include work, education, family, friendships, community organizations, public services, recreation, and online communities.
2. Do not force a setting that does not fit the value pair.
3. Vary the protagonist’s role, relationships, decision object, practical constraints, and stakes.
4. Do not treat changes in names, locations, or quantities as sufficient to create a new scenario.
5. Avoid repeatedly using the same underlying dilemma with different surface details.
6. Do not associate one sub-value systematically with a particular gender, age group, social status, or type of person.
7. Check the entire set for substantive repetition before returning it.

ACTION OPTION REQUIREMENTS

For each scenario:

1. Provide exactly two action options.
2. Assign option_id "v1" to the option primarily expressing Sub-value 1.
3. Assign option_id "v2" to the option primarily expressing Sub-value 2.
4. Write each option as one concrete action statement with an omitted subject, such as “Allocate the available funding equally between the two groups.”
5. Make the options comparable in length, specificity, tone, and level of commitment.
6. Ensure both options address the same decision at the same point in time.
7. Make each option prioritize its target sub-value without caricaturing or condemning the other.
8. Do not include value labels, theoretical terminology, moral praise, or moral criticism in the option text.
9. Do not introduce facts, resources, permissions, or guaranteed outcomes that were not established in the scenario.
10. Do not add a third option, a compromise option, or an “insufficient information” option.
11. When a sub-value concerns an emotion or internal state, describe a feasible action that expresses or supports it. Do not require the protagonist to instantly produce an involuntary feeling.

PERSPECTIVE REQUIREMENTS

1. First establish the third-person scenario and its two options.
2. Derive the second-person scenario directly from that third-person scenario.
3. Replace references to the protagonist with “you,” “your,” or the appropriate corresponding form.
4. Change only protagonist references and grammatically necessary agreement.
5. Preserve the same:
- Roles and relationships.
- Other characters and their names.
- Events and sequence.
- Quantities and timing.
- Available information.
- Constraints and uncertainty.
- Explicitly stated thoughts and feelings.
6. Do not add instructions such as “Imagine yourself in this situation.”
7. Do not add emotional language, personal involvement, or background information to either version.
8. Use “What should [protagonist’s name] do?” for the third-person question.
9. Use “What should you do?” for the second-person question.
10. Use the exact same two action-option texts for both versions. Store them once in shared_options.
11. Keep option identities and their value mappings unchanged across perspectives.

QUALITY CHECK

Before returning the dataset, check that:

- There are exactly 20 distinct underlying situations.
- Each scenario supports both sub-values within the upper value.
- The two options express distinguishable priorities.
- The trade-off follows from the stated situation.
- Both options are feasible and reasonably defensible.
- Neither option introduces unsupported information.
- Neither wording nor option length systematically favors one sub-value.
- Each perspective pair preserves all substantive content.
- No scenario is merely a renamed or renumbered duplicate.

Revise items that fail these checks before returning them.

Do not output your drafting process.

OUTPUT FORMAT

Return one valid JSON object and no surrounding commentary or Markdown.

The top-level fields must be:

- status: "complete" or "needs_definition"
- issue: an empty string if complete; otherwise a concise explanation
- value_pair: the supplied value pair
- items: an array of exactly 20 objects if complete; otherwise an empty array

Each item must contain:

- item_id: a string from "01" through "20"
- third_person:
- scenario: the third-person scenario text
- question: the third-person question
- second_person:
- scenario: the matched second-person scenario text
- question: the second-person question
- shared_options:
- An object containing option_id "v1" and its text
- An object containing option_id "v2" and its text
- researcher_metadata:
- setting: a brief setting label
- decision_summary: a one-sentence description of the decision
- tradeoff: a one-sentence explanation of the situational constraint
- v1_alignment: a one-sentence explanation of how option v1 expresses Sub-value 1
- v2_alignment: a one-sentence explanation of how option v2 expresses Sub-value 2

Keep researcher_metadata separate from the scenario, question, and action-option text. It is for researcher review and must not be included in the material presented to respondents.
